In one minute

How checkable answers become a review workflow a team runs daily: unsure cases go to people, and prompts ship with canary release and rollback.

I turned Grounded Review into a four-step review workflow: the model decides, evidence-check verifies the quotes, ads that fail the check or that the model is unsure of go to a human review queue, and reviewed ads come back as regression items; plus a policy retrieval evaluation, prompt versions, canary and rollback rules, weekly reports and a launch checklist, with the review queue syncable to Lark Base.

Letting the model say it is unsure reduces wrong decisions that go live at the cost of more human review; the canary rolled back under the rules written beforehand, which exposed mistakes in the rules themselves; these are reviewed and production stays at v1.

What I assumed

Expected a version meeting all four adoption criteria to roll out smoothly

What the data showed

It rolled back at 10% under the rules; the fault was in the rules themselves, so production stays at v1

  • 10% Canary share where the rules rolled it back

Limits

01Traffic replays test items rather than real users, and reviewer decisions are the items' fixed answers, not real reviewers

Recommendation: After launch, track wrong decisions let through and review volume per version on real requests, and feed QA samples back as regression items.

02v2 lets fewer wrong decisions through, but human review rose from 6/332 to 72/332, often on clear ads the model called unsure

Recommendation: Next, reduce “unsure” on clear ads, again through a regression test before a canary.

03Two rollback rules were wrong

the fixed rules were written after seeing the results, so reaching full release on the same requests is not evidence. Recommendation: Align rollback rules with the change’s purpose and adoption criteria, check only hard conditions on small steps, and rerun the canary on newly written items.
