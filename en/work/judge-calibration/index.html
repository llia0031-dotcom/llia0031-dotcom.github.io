Operations

In one minute

A model judge is convenient, but how far can it be trusted, which checks can it take, which belong to a script, and which must stay with people?

I measured one model judge against three standards kept apart: answers fixed when the items were built, regulator and court decisions, and the human ratings that ship with public datasets; then set which checks go to a script, a judge or a person, and built a quality-check agent with an acceptance standard. The plan, frozen files and predictions were written before the runs.

A model judge can be trusted on checks with clear answers that trace back to the text, and rule-checkable parts are safer with a script; decisions that turn on outside evidence and subjective quality ratings stay far from people and should stay with them. The judge’s own “unsure” marks the items it gets wrong most often, which makes it a good signal for sending items to people.

What the data showed

Padding changed 0/368 and assurances 2/368; no wrong answer got through

  • 6/11 Regulator decisions that turn on outside evidence: right 54.5%, 95% CI 28.0 to 78.7%
  • 2/390 Errors missed with rules, judge and review (judge alone: 7/390) 0.5%, 95% CI 0.1 to 1.9%

Limits

01The items come from the same set of projects' stored answers and the errors are planted

“argued to a wrong conclusion” samples were produced by `eval/gen_flips.py` with the same model, so a bias towards its own writing is not ruled out. Recommendation: Before use, calibrate again on real records with settled outcomes, with error samples from other sources.

02Only 46 regulator and court decisions, 11 of them turning on outside evidence

the intervals are wide. Recommendation: Leave such decisions to people, or gather the outside evidence before handing them to a judge.

03The acceptance standard passed only 86.6% of two-percent batches, short of the at-least-nine-in-ten target set beforehand

ten-percent batches passed 9.4%, meeting theirs. The agent’s false flags blur the flag rate, and looking back, no flag-rate threshold alone meets both targets. Recommendation: Count only flags confirmed on review towards the threshold (not yet tested), then test again on simulated batches.
