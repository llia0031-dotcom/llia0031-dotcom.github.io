How it was measured

  • The questions: 240 messages, 120 in Chinese and 120 in English, written from the site’s material without sight of the rules, the prepared question wordings or the prompt; every label checked once more, then frozen. They cover the common questions, the projects, details only on a project page, follow-up chains, private matters, things the site does not cover, manipulation and injection, rudeness and small talk.
  • Both versions answer the same set. The rules version finds a written answer in the browser. The model version answers small talk, rudeness, private matters and out-of-scope requests with the rules when the rules recognise them, and sends the rest to claude-haiku-5-5, which answers only from the site’s material and cites the passages it draws on.
  • Grading: each reply is graded twice, independently, against its label and the whole material; the two gradings agree on 92% or more, and where they differ the lower one counts. Saying anything the material does not support counts as wrong.
  • The “With the model” column is the version frozen before the questions were written, measured once. The “After fixes” column was measured after four rounds of changes on the same questions: on 2026-11-23, prompt and code fixes from a code review and these gradings, such as no longer adding “we can talk in person” on its own; on 2026-11-25, three more rounds of prompt changes so that Chinese replies carry no English words, and a rewritten reply to “is this a real person”. After that, the rules learned more ways of asking who is answering, and the model’s material gained an entry on how this box answers. No question in the set moved to a written reply because of it; the model’s replies were not rerun with the new material, and only the two asking whether a real person is typing were graded again against it. Those questions had already been used, so it is not a blind test.
  • First-word times and costs were measured calling the API directly from the development machine. Through the live Worker, the first words reach the page in 0.7 to 1.5 seconds; the first question in each language, which has to fill the cache, is the slowest.

For reference, on another question set, used to tune the rules but never the model: rules 70% right, with the model 83.8%.

Where it falls short

  • Counted by characters, about 24% of the text carries no source: the largest part is whole sentences saying the site does not cover something or that it is out of scope, then joining phrases; the rest is whole sentences without a source, mostly naming the matching project or restating the site’s material.
  • On things the site does not cover, a quarter of the replies still fall short, mostly by not saying so plainly.
  • Some networks, mainland China’s among them, may not reach workers.dev; the page then answers with the rules version.
